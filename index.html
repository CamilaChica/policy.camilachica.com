02
Working Paper · CPI–WP–2026–01

Peace-Building in Post-Conflict Democracies: Lessons from Colombia for American Policy

This working paper examines Colombia’s 2016 peace accord implementation as a case study in institutional resilience, drawing direct lessons for United States foreign policy engagement in conflict-affected regions. The paper argues that sustainable peace requires not merely cessation of hostilities but active institutional investment in transitional justice, civil society empowerment, and inter-communal dialogue — frameworks equally applicable to domestic polarization challenges facing the United States.

Building on the author’s participation at the International Congress on Peace and Development in Geneva (September 2023) and the Corporación Unificada Nacional peace conferences, this paper synthesizes academic literature with firsthand policy observation.

About the Founder
05
Principal Researcher

Camila A.
Chica Ortiz

Political Scientist · Policy Analyst · Civic Advocate

Camila A. Chica Ortiz is a political scientist, author, and civic advocate whose research sits at the intersection of democratic governance, religious freedom, and peace diplomacy. A proud United States citizen with deep Colombian roots, she brings a uniquely bicultural and comparative perspective to American policy questions.

Her work has been informed by direct participation in international policy forums — including the United Nations-affiliated ILPES-CEPAL program in Santiago, the International Congress in Geneva, and peace-building conferences across Colombia. She has also served in active electoral campaign roles, bringing academic research into practical democratic engagement.
